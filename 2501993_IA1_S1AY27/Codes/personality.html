Personality

My Personality Traits

Determination

One of my strongest traits is determination. I have seen this in myself especially since starting university. My first year at UTech did not go exactly how I wanted it to, especially with Programming 2. I had a missing lab test that affected my course, and having to deal with that was frustrating because I knew I was capable of doing better. Instead of deciding that computing was not for me, I started thinking about how I could fix the situation. I planned to retake the course and make sure I understood the material before moving further into my degree. Even now, when I have several lectures, assignments, and tutorials to catch up on, I usually make a list and work through them one by one. I have learned that determination for me is not about never struggling. It is about still trying after I do.

Ambition

I would also describe myself as ambitious. I do not want to go to university just to say that I have a degree. I actually think about what I want my life to look like after university. Choosing computing was a big part of that because I want to build a career in technology and eventually become financially independent. I also push myself to take opportunities outside of school. For example, I have looked into work opportunities and programs that could give me experience and help me become more independent. I sometimes put a lot of pressure on myself because I want to know that I am making progress, but that same ambition is what keeps me thinking about my future when things at university become stressful.

Creativity

Lastly, I consider myself creative. I think this is something that shows up in my everyday life, not just in school. I enjoy expressing myself through my appearance, planning outfits and hairstyles, and thinking about the kind of content I want to create. I have even thought about creating university diary content because I like the idea of turning ordinary parts of my life into something creative that other people can enjoy. I also bring this creativity into school. When I am struggling to understand something, I usually do not want to simply memorize it. I try to break it down, make notes in a way that makes sense to me, practice questions, and explain it back to myself. Creativity has therefore helped me find ways of learning that fit me instead of forcing myself to learn exactly like everyone else.

My Hero

The person I look up to most is Queen Charlotte from Queen Charlotte: A Bridgerton Story. I know she is a fictional character, but her story stood out to me because I connected with the person she became rather than just the fact that she was a queen.

What I admire about her is how she grows into herself. She is put into a completely unfamiliar situation and has to deal with expectations, responsibilities, and circumstances that she did not choose. At first, she has to figure out how to navigate everything around her, but she eventually becomes someone who is confident in herself and knows what she deserves.

A scene that stands out to me is the moment when she makes it clear that she is not going to simply accept being treated as though her feelings do not matter. That part of her character resonates with me because I have also had experiences where I had to learn to speak up for myself and recognize what I deserve from the people around me.

"I will stay. I command it."

I also relate to her because I am at a point in my life where I am becoming more independent and figuring out who I want to be. I am trying to build my future through university and my career while also figuring out myself outside of academics. I have had situations that did not turn out the way I expected, but they have taught me more about myself. What I take from Queen Charlotte is that becoming stronger does not happen all at once. Sometimes you grow because life puts you in situations where you have no choice but to learn, adapt, and become more confident in yourself.

LLMs as a Tool, Not a Crutch

The statement that LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot must be used as a tool, not a crutch, means to me that I can use them to help me understand something, but I should not depend on them to do the entirety of my work. Although I agree that LLMs can assist students, the article AI is Not "Just a Tool" by Brett's Codes, published on September 19, 2026, made me think differently about the idea of calling AI "just a tool." The author explains that LLMs are more complicated than ordinary tools because they can make mistakes and their use can affect the development of important skills.

As a student, I believe LLMs can be useful in many parts of my university life. For example, when I am doing a programming assignment and I come across a problem in my code, I could send ChatGPT a picture of my code and ask it to identify the line causing the problem, explain what is wrong and tell me how I can solve it. I could also use it when I do not understand how to do something by asking it to teach me how to do it rather than simply asking it to do it for me.

Another way I could use LLMs is to help me understand my lecture notes. I could ask it to summarize long notes, explain a difficult topic using simpler words or create a study routine to help me go through the material. This is useful because everyone does not learn in the same way. Sometimes a lecturer can explain something and I still may not understand it, but with an LLM I can explain exactly how I want the information to be presented. I can ask for examples, simpler explanations or step-by-step instructions until the concept makes sense to me. This allows the LLM to support my learning rather than replace it.

However, I do not think I should allow an LLM to become my crutch. One reason is that AI can make mistakes. If I depend completely on AI without knowing the subject myself, I may accept incorrect information without realizing that it is wrong. If I am actually learning in class, I am more likely to notice when an AI gives me an incorrect answer and question it. I can then correct the information instead of blindly accepting it. This connects to Brett's Codes' point that an ordinary tool does not normally make mistakes in the way an LLM can. Therefore, I need to understand what I am doing while using AI for assistance.

I also do not want to use AI to complete an entire project by simply copying and pasting without understanding anything for myself. As a Computing student, I am preparing for a career where I will actually be expected to use the skills I am learning. If I graduate with a degree but cannot perform something I was supposed to learn in first year, how can I prove that I genuinely earned that degree? If an employer asks me to complete something and I have to rely on AI for a skill I should already have, my degree alone will not demonstrate what I can actually do.

Therefore, I believe I should use AI as a personal tutor rather than a replacement for my own thinking. I can use it to support, explain and improve my work, but I should still be responsible for learning the material myself. To me, an LLM is an effective means of learning when I know enough to question it, correct it and understand what it is teaching me rather than simply accepting everything it says.
