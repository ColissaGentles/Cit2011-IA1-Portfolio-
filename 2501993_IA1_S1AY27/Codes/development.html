7 Cures for a Lean Purse

I read "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" and "The Five Laws of Gold" by George S. Clason's The Richest Man in Babylon which made me think about money a lot more deeply than I thought. The lessons were not new to me as I grew up seeing people say things like "save your money" and "don't spend more than you have." But the chapters made me think about them differently, because I could relate them to my own experience as a university student. I don't have a lot of money and also have to think very carefully about transportation, food, school and personal needs. Without thinking about what I am doing with it, money disappears as quickly because I am not being intentional about what I am doing.

The first cure, "Start thy purse to fattening," struck me because Clason advises people to save at least one-tenth of what they make. My first thought was that saving can be difficult when you have a lot of things to pay for. But I realized that waiting until I have "enough" money to save could be a bad habit. There will probably always be something else I could spend money on. Saving has to be something I make room for instead of something I do only when there's money left over.

I have experienced this with my own budgeting. I have had to figure out with a limited budget how I would use my money for a few things. For instance, when I had about J$12,000 and had to spend J$5,000 on a calculator for university, I came to realize how quickly one purchase can consume so much of what I have. And then I had the food and other expenses. Things like this have taught me to not treat all of my money as spending money. If I don't save a huge sum of money, I can put something aside and get the habit of saving and have something to go back on and back up.

The second cure, "Control thy expenditures," was more so. Clason says people need to distinguish between their needs and their wants. As a student, I have to make this distinction every day. Transportation, food, school materials, and other university expenses are things I need. But there are things that I may want just because I like them or because I feel like buying them right now.

What I learned is that being very careful about spending does not mean I can never enjoy spending money. It means I need to be more conscious of my choices. In fact, a small purchase might not seem like much, but a few small purchases can add up. If I spend money on things I want and don't have enough to cover a lot of the little, essential stuff I really do, I have made it so embarrassing for myself. I want to be better at thinking about what I will need my money for before I spend it.

"Make thy gold multiply" also made me think about how I could build on what I currently have to make something bigger in the future. Now I'm not having a lot of money, but I can invest in myself. It is my education that's the biggest investment I am going to be making. Computing is my goal because I want to learn programming, web development, problem solving, databases, and other technical skills to get money in my hands now, but they can also open the door in the future.

This relates to the seventh cure, "Increase thy ability to earn." I appreciated that because saving is a finite resource, and you just can't do it alone. There's only so much you can cut from your expenses. And at some point, I will need to get into earning more money. For me, that means going back to school, working hard, and building skills in ways that will be useful. I don't want to graduate with a degree only. I want to graduate with skills that I can actually use in the workplace.

"The more of wisdom we know, the more we may earn. That man who seeks to learn more of his craft shall be richly rewarded."

5 Laws of Gold

In "The Five Laws of Gold," we learned that money is not enough if you don't understand how to use it right. Nomasir's experiences illustrated that gold is easily lost when a person doesn't know how to use it properly. I learned to not be attracted to opportunities just because they promise quick money. If I do invest, I want to know what I'm doing before I proceed.

The law regarding seeking advice from experienced people also struck me. I believe that people are reluctant to admit that they don't understand something, especially if it's money. I would rather be educated and ask questions than make a decision based on assumptions. Just as I would ask for help if I don't understand something in one of my Computing courses, I should be willing to seek reliable financial advice when I need it.

My Biggest Takeaway

The lesson learned from both chapters is that wisdom is more valuable than money itself. Money can be earned and lost or stolen, but knowledge can be learned, and that knowledge can lead to better decisions in life too. I could easily waste my money if I suddenly had much more money but still had poor spending habits that were not wise and could easily be mismanaged. If I learn how to do money management with less money when I have less, I will be able to be more efficient with my decisions.

The chapters also made me think about what kind of financial future I want. I want to be independent and able to handle my own responsibilities. I do not want every unexpected expense to make me panic because I have nothing saved. I want to be able to enjoy what I earn with the knowledge that I'm going to make money in the future. That is to say, my present need is going to be balanced with my future needs.

"Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" and "The Five Laws of Gold" were all the proof that financial success is not only about how much money we earn. It is also about how we make use of that money. As a student, I may not have a lot of money right now, but I can develop the habits I need later on. I can save when I can, control spending, invest in education, protect my money, learn to learn, and make money. That's the biggest thing I have learned from these chapters: that I am not going to do this until I have a little money. I want to learn now, so when I do earn more, I will know how to keep it, protect it, grow it, and use it in order to create the independence and the future I want.
